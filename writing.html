Study charts

Ways of praying the psalms.

A condensed guide adapted from the psalm-types chart in Prayer in Song, chapter 3. These categories overlap; they highlight different ways the psalms give voice to prayer.

TypeFocusExample
PraiseGlorifying God for who He is and what He has done.Psalm 103
WisdomReflecting on God's law and the way of righteous living.Psalm 119
PenitentialExpressing sorrow for sin and asking for mercy.Psalm 51
LamentSpeaking honestly from suffering while continuing to address God.Psalm 22
ProcessionalPrayer associated with pilgrimage, movement, and communal worship.Psalm 132

Source: Marcus Larsen, Prayer in Song, chapter 3, manuscript pages 17–18. Biblical numbering follows the Hebrew tradition.

Isaiah's vision.

A developing hymn inspired by Isaiah 6: divine holiness, repentance, cleansing, and the call to service. This excerpt is the closing Trinitarian stanza.

Draft text by Marcus Larsen · September 2026
Holy art Thou, O Father of creation. Holy Thy Son, the author of salvation. Holy Thy Spirit. Praise and adoration now and forever.

Teaching through hymnody.

My hymn-study work brings historical context and theological reading into conversation with familiar texts. The goal is to help people understand what they sing, notice the Scripture behind it, and approach hymnody as a form of prayer.
